The basics

Central Akita is reached by Shinkansen or by air from Tokyo, then by a short local railway into the mountains. Nothing is far once you're here, but connections are infrequent, so it pays to plan ahead.

By trainTokyo → Kakunodate
Akita Shinkansen "Komachi", about 3 hours. Covered by the JR Pass.
By airHaneda → Odate-Noshiro
About 1 hr 10 min with ANA. Shared taxis connect to Mt. Moriyoshi.
Local railwayAkita Nairiku Line
Runs from Kakunodate to Takanosu through the mountains. The JR Pass is not valid on this line.

Tip: trains and shared taxis in rural Akita are infrequent, often just a handful of runs a day. Check timetables and book ahead before you go.

Getting to Mt. Moriyoshi

There are two main ways to reach the snow monsters: save time by flying, or save money and enjoy the scenery by train. There is no bus to the ski resort — either way, the last stretch is a shared taxi you book in advance.

Option A · FastestFly + shared taxi

Tokyo Haneda → Odate-Noshiro Airport (about 1 hr 10 min), then a shared taxi straight to the ski resort (about 1 hr 20 min). Higher cost: the taxi is ¥11,500 per adult.

Option B · Scenic & cheaperShinkansen + local train + shared taxi

Tokyo → Kakunodate by Shinkansen (about 3 hrs), the Akita Nairiku Line to Aniai (about 1.5 hrs), then a 25-min shared taxi (¥2,300). Longer, but the train ride through snowy countryside is an experience in itself.

Option B step by step

  1. 1Akita Shinkansen · about 3 hrs

    Tokyo → Kakunodate

    Covered by the JR Pass. Kakunodate, an old samurai town, makes a good place to stay the night before.

  2. 2Akita Nairiku Line · about 1.5 hrs

    Kakunodate → Aniai Station

    The Nairiku Line station is right next to JR Kakunodate Station. The JR Pass is not valid on this line. A one-day pass (about ¥2,500) is sold at station windows.

  3. 3Moriyoshi Shuttle (shared taxi) · about 25 min

    Aniai Station → Ani Ski Resort

    ¥2,300 adult / ¥1,150 child one way (fares from Nov 1, 2026). Book at least 90 minutes before departure, online (NearMe, prepaid) or by phone with Ani Taxi: 0186-82-3115.

  4. 4Ani Gondola · about 20 min

    Base station → Summit station (1,167 m)

    Walk 5 minutes to the snow-monster field.

Plan the return first. Trains from Aniai are infrequent, so check the Nairiku Line timetable and pick the ride back that connects best. The last gondola down is at 15:30, so the 16:00 shuttle is the last of the day.

Option A: shared taxi from the airport

ANA flies from Tokyo Haneda to Odate-Noshiro Airport (about 1 hr 10 min). A shared taxi runs from the airport and JR Takanosu Station straight to the ski resort, all year round. Book at least 2 hours ahead. ¥11,500 adult / ¥5,750 child one way (fares from Nov 1, 2026).

Getting to Kakunodate

Kakunodate is the simplest place to reach in central Akita, and it's where the Akita Nairiku Line begins — making it a natural first or last stop on a trip north.

From TokyoAbout 3 hours
Akita Shinkansen "Komachi", covered by the JR Pass
Station → samurai districtAbout 15–20 min walk

Combine the two: spend a night in Kakunodate, then take the morning Nairiku Line train north to Mt. Moriyoshi. See Option B above for the connecting times.

Good to know

  • Trains and shuttles are infrequent. Plan your return before you leave, and build in slack around connections.
  • Shared taxis must be booked ahead — 90 minutes ahead for the Moriyoshi Shuttle, 2 hours ahead for the airport shuttle.
  • The JR Pass does not cover the Akita Nairiku Line or any shared-taxi shuttle. A one-day Nairiku Line pass (about ¥2,500) covers unlimited rides.
  • Dress for serious cold in winter. The Moriyoshi summit is around 1,200 m and exposed to the wind — proper boots, gloves, a hat and layers.
  • Little English is spoken locally. A translation app helps. Station staff and taxi drivers are friendly, and booking through a tourist information center can make things easier.
